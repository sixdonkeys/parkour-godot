# Decision: player-body

- **Options:**
  1. A kinematic body: the engine's `CharacterBody3D` moved by `move_and_slide`, which already gives slopes, floor snapping and moving platforms. Step-up comes from adopting Andicraft's `stairs-character` (MIT). The momentum model is written on top, using what the exemplar controllers do well.
  2. A physics-driven body: a `RigidBody3D` pushed by forces (the engine demo `3d/rigidbody_character`). Momentum and pushes come free, but the solver decides the motion, so the same input need not give the same jump.
  3. Adopt one exemplar controller whole: Jeh3no's state-machine controller (MIT) is the fullest. It reads the global `Input`, takes its direction from the camera holder and runs timers in `_process`, so most of it would have to be rewritten to fit this build's rules.
  4. Write a kinematic solver from `move_and_collide` and `body_test_motion` alone (the Quake way). Full control, but it re-makes what `move_and_slide` already does and has survived real use.
- **Sources:** `docs/survey/player-body.md` — class reference 4.7.2 for `CharacterBody3D`, `SeparationRayShape3D`, `AnimatableBody3D` and the Jolt motion-query settings (github.com/godotengine/godot, tag `4.7.2-stable`); Andicraft `stairs-character`; Jeh3no's advanced first-person controller; DeiranZ's parkour controller; the NOSHOT controller; the GDQuest third-person controller (script only); Godot's demo projects `3d/platformer` and `3d/physics_interpolation`. All fetched 2026-11-15, each licence read from its own `LICENSE` file.
- **Choice:** option 1. `PlayerBody` (`game/movement/player_body.gd`) extends `StairsCharacter`, which extends `CharacterBody3D`.
  - **Kinematic.** One `move_and_slide` per tick, at 120 ticks a second, reading only the body's `InputSource`. Nothing in `_process` touches the simulation.
  - **Shape.** An upright cylinder, 1.8 m tall and 0.3 m in radius, named `Collider`. A flat bottom stands on a ledge up to its edge, so a landing either makes the ledge or misses it, and take-off points are exact. A capsule's round bottom slides off edges. `stairs-character` was tested by its author with Jolt and cylinders.
  - **Sharp edges.** Jolt rounds the edges of a box or cylinder by its `Shape3D.margin` (the class reference says so; the default is 0.04 m). A rounded ledge gives a body that overlaps it by a few centimetres a slanted contact, which reads as a wall, not a floor. The body's cylinder and every box a body runs on use a margin of 0.002 m. This is a rule for `kit` and the levels, not only for the fixture.
  - **Slopes.** `floor_max_angle` 46° (a 45° ramp is floor, with a degree in hand for float error), `floor_constant_speed` on, `floor_stop_on_slope` on, `floor_snap_length` 0.35 m (the step height) while grounded and 0 from take-off until the body is falling (Jeh3no does the same). Measured: with `floor_constant_speed` the body's horizontal speed on a slope is what it is on the flat, so a route's length on the map is what it costs to run, and the speed along the slope itself is higher by 1/cos of the angle.
  - **Steps.** `stairs-character`: when the horizontal move is blocked, test up by the step height, forward, then down, with `PhysicsServer3D.body_test_motion`, and lift the body only if it lands on walkable ground. Step height 0.35 m. Going down, the engine's floor snap (same length) keeps the body on stairs and curbs instead of launching it. Two changes were made to the adopted script, each a commit of its own (`docs/upstream/stairs-character.md`): the forward test reaches at least 0.1 m onto the step, without which a body that first touched a riser at the end of a tick's motion stopped dead at a step it could climb; and the test objects are reused. The body keeps its own ground state across a step-up, because the engine's floor flag can read false for a tick right after one; without that, stairs fired `left_ground` and `landed`.
  - **Moving surfaces.** `AnimatableBody3D` whose transform is a pure function of the world tick (`MovingSurface`, `game/movement/moving_surface.gd`; the tick comes from `WorldClock`, `game/core/world_clock.gd`). `move_and_slide` carries the body on them. `platform_on_leave` stays at "add velocity", so a jump off a moving surface keeps its speed (DeiranZ adds `get_platform_velocity()` by hand for the same effect). A body that lands on a moving surface has the surface's velocity taken off its own, so its speed is from then on measured against what it stands on. Two rules for `kit` came out of the fuzz run: a body is carried one tick behind the surface (4 cm at 5 m/s), and a moving surface must never close a gap narrower than a body against fixed geometry, because being pushed out of the surface can push the body into the wall behind it (seen once, when the fixture put a body inside the lift; spawn points on a moving surface now ride with it).
  - **Momentum.** Horizontal velocity is state, and input steers it; it is never set outright.
    - On the ground it accelerates quickly to run speed and then builds slowly to top speed. Speed built is kept through jumps, landings, steps and slides along walls.
    - A turn rotates the velocity toward the wish direction and sheds only the sideways part, at the grip rate. Letting go brakes at the brake rate.
    - In the air nothing slows the body. Steering can turn or shorten the velocity, and can add speed only up to the air-control cap, so air steering cannot be farmed for speed.
    - Walls take only the part of the velocity that points into them (`move_and_slide`).
  - **Jump.** Jeh3no's model: a height, a time to the peak and a time to fall give the take-off speed and two gravities. Coyote time and a jump buffer are counted in ticks. Releasing jump early raises gravity while the body is still rising (the Godot platformer demo cuts the jump on release). Gravity is integrated with the mid-tick velocity, so a jump follows the exact parabola whatever the tick rate; that is what lets the jump instrument be proved against arithmetic.
  - **Independent of any camera.** The body owns `yaw` and `pitch` as simulation state. Each tick they take the source's `look_x` and `look_y` (radians) plus the look-stick actions. The wish direction is the move input rotated by `yaw`. A camera rig reads `yaw` and `pitch` and the body's interpolated transform; the body never reads a camera. The Godot interpolation demo is built the same way round.
  - **More than one body.** Bodies are made by `PlayerBody.spawn(slot)` from a `PlayerSlot`; nothing is a singleton. World collision is layer 1, players are layer 2. A ghost sits on no layer and doesn't scan layer 2, so it collides with the world and with no player.
  - **Events and state.** `state` is `GROUNDED` or `AIRBORNE` (traversal adds its own). `jumped`, `left_ground` and `landed(speed)` fire on the tick they happen; `landed` carries the downward speed at contact.
  - **What was not taken.** NOSHOT's step is a ray ahead of the body and a teleport; it has no test that the body fits where it is put. Jeh3no's and DeiranZ's movement lerps velocity toward a target each tick, which bleeds momentum on every turn, and both read `Input` and a camera directly.
- **Reason:** the brief wants every move to respond at once and fail only when the player erred, a recorded run that replays, and a body that survives ten minutes of random input. A kinematic body gives all three: the same input gives the same motion, and the motion is whatever the code says. The engine's `move_and_slide` on Jolt has had far more use than anything written here would, and `stairs-character` fills its one gap with a tested method (shape tests, not a ray). Option 2 hands the feel to the solver. Option 3 would keep little of the adopted code once input, camera and timers were moved out. Option 4 is option 1 with more to get wrong.
